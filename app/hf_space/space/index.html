How it works

  • Inputs: interplanetary magnetic field (|B|, Bx, By, Bz), solar-wind speed, density and temperature, and F10.7 solar flux. No geomagnetic indices are used, so the nowcast does not wait for ground magnetometers.
  • Features: 66 physics-based features: coupling functions (Newell, V·Bs, electric field), pressure, lags, 3–24 h trailing statistics, position inside the 3-hour Kp block, and time of day and year.
  • Model: scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting, chosen over Linear Regression, KNN, SVR and Random Forest by time-ordered cross-validation. Quantile models with a conformal correction give the 80% range.
  • Data: NASA OMNI2 hourly solar wind (1998–2025) with GFZ Potsdam definitive Kp as labels. Live inputs come from NOAA SWPC's propagated real-time solar wind.
  • Leakage safeguards: causal features only, the same feature code for training and live use (unit-tested), decisions made on validation years only, and the test years scored once per model version.

Limitations

  • The largest storms (G3+) are under-predicted by about 0.6 Kp on average, because they are rare in the training data.
  • Sudden storm onsets show up 1–2 hours late within a 3-hour Kp block.
  • Lead time is at most about 1 hour, the travel time of the solar wind to Earth.
  • For education and awareness only, not an operational warning service. Use NOAA SWPC or GFZ for that.
